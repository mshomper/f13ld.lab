# F13LD.foam stiffness calibration — study design (v0.14.0)

**Date:** 2026-10-02 · **Status (2026-10-03):** first pass fitted (§6–7) and live in F13LD.foam v0.5.0 (§9); **provisional** — 27 runs stopped at the old 300-iteration cap, re-run list ready (§10); cell-count pass ready (§8) · **Plan:** [`FOAM_CALIBRATION_HANDOFF.md`](FOAM_CALIBRATION_HANDOFF.md) (approved with defaults)

Goal: fit F13LD.foam's in-tool stiffness estimate to the lab's validated FFT solver, per topology, rather than borrowing literature fits.

## 1. How to run it

1. Open F13LD.lab (v0.14.0+) → **⟳ Sweep** → CSV tab → load [`foam-calibration/foam_calibration_runs.csv`](foam-calibration/foam_calibration_runs.csv) (48 runs).
2. Settings: precision **Standard (1e-4)** · void **1e-6** · second grid **64 ↔ 128 pair** · order 2. Standard, not High: on the first 8 rows (A-open 8–25 %, Matt's GPU, 2026-10-02) Standard and High agreed to three significant figures on all six moduli, at a third of the iterations. A CPU trace of one load case (disordered open foam, 32³) shows why: the stiffness is within 0.1 % of its final value once the residual reaches ~1e-4, and it creeps with the residual below that, so 1e-5 buys little. v0.15.0's fast solver path (16i) makes each run several times cheaper on top of that.
3. Run all → **Export CSV**. Send the results CSV back; the fit (§5) joins it to the run CSV by `run_id`.

The run CSV is regenerated by `node docs/foam-calibration/make_runs.js`. Seeds are regenerated from the generator settings with the foam tool's own `FoamSeeds` code, so each row is the foam F13LD.foam shows for those settings. Each row's thickness is solved so the voxel solid fraction at 64³ (before island trim) hits the target; the fit uses each run's **measured** solid fraction, not the target.

## 2. Resolution — why the cell counts are 27 and 16

All foams use normalize on, so the wall (closed) or strut (open, plateau) is 2·thickness across, in tile units (tile = 10). Voxels across that width at 64³ / 128³:

| topology · cells | ρ 8 % | 12 % | 18 % | 25 % | 35 % |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| open · 50 | 2.4 / 4.7 | 3.0 / 6.0 | 3.8 / 7.6 | 4.6 / 9.2 | 5.8 / 11.5 |
| **open · 27** | **2.9 / 5.9** | 3.7 / 7.4 | 4.7 / 9.3 | 5.7 / 11.4 | 7.1 / 14.2 |
| closed · 50 | 0.5 / 1.0 | 0.8 / 1.6 | 1.2 / 2.4 | 1.7 / 3.5 | 2.5 / 5.0 |
| **closed · 16** | 0.8 / 1.5 | **1.1 / 2.3** | 1.8 / 3.5 | 2.5 / 5.0 | 3.7 / 7.4 |

(Plateau k 0.05 tracks open within 0.01.) Closed-cell walls are thin: at the same density a wall carries the mass of a whole face, so it is several times thinner than an open strut. Choices that follow:

- **Open and plateau: 27 cells per tile, 8–35 %.** Fine at 128³ throughout.
- **Closed: 16 cells per tile, 12–35 %.** 8 % is left out (1.5 voxels across at 128³). The 12 % rows are 2.3 voxels across at 128³ and carry a "thin" note — read them as indicative. Kelvin closed at 12 % (2.0) and Weaire–Phelan closed at 12 % (2.7) likewise.
- Stiffness depends on ρ, not on how many cells share the tile, so a law fitted at 16–27 cells transfers to the 50–800 the foam tool makes. The foam tool's cell slider starts at 50; the lab sweeps any count.
- The lab's partial-volume voxel item (NEXT_STEPS §1a.3) would help closed foams most.

## 4. CPU spot checks (this session, before the GPU run)

CPU reference (`homogenizeFullCPU`, void 1e-4, tolerance 1e-4) at **32³**, open, 27 cells, Lloyd, one realization. A 32³ solve took 5–9 minutes on the session container, so only three were run.

| ρ | struts across at 32³ | E/Es (mean of x, y, z) | G/Es | ν | Gibson–Ashby ρ² |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 12 % | 1.8 voxels | 0.00042 | 0.00013 | 0.33 | 0.0144 |
| 18 % | 2.3 | 0.0099 | 0.0034 | 0.36 | 0.0324 |
| 30 % | 3.2 | 0.0587 | 0.0220 | 0.29 | 0.090 |

What this does and doesn't show:

- **It doesn't measure the law.** The apparent exponent is ~8 between 12 and 18 % and ~3.5 between 18 and 30 % — at 1.8–2.3 voxels across, struts lose voxel contact at the nodes and stiffness collapses. That is a resolution effect and is why the study uses the 64 ↔ 128 pair with ≥ 3 voxels across where possible.
- At 30 % (3.2 voxels) the lab reads 0.65× Gibson–Ashby's ρ². A plausible share of that is real: this field puts extra mass at the nodes, where it adds little bending stiffness. The GPU study will separate the two.
- G/Es ≈ 0.31–0.37 × E/Es (Gibson–Ashby: 3/8) and ν ≈ 0.3, as expected for isotropic open foams. The ~±10 % spread between x, y and z is one realization of a 27-cell disordered tile; set A's two realizations measure it.
- The import path, field parity with mesh (≤ 6e-14), exact solid-fraction targeting and the CSV loader are checked by `validate-foam.js` and by loading this CSV through the lab's own parser (45 of 48 rows build within 0.5 % of their target at 64³. The ordered closed lattices have many tied field values, so their thickness can't split them exactly: Kelvin closed 12 % builds at 13.5 %, Weaire–Phelan closed 12 % at 12.4 % and 25 % at 24.8 %. The CSV's `expected_vf_pct` is what each row actually builds, so the lab's build check stays exact, and `target_vf_pct` keeps the intent).

## 5. After the run — fit and estimator (next session)

1. Per topology: E/Es = C·ρⁿ on the directional mean (extrapolated 64 ↔ 128 where both grids converge; 128 alone for the thin closed rows), plus G/Es and ν. The scatter across realizations and seed modes becomes the "likely range" band.
2. Stretch (set D) → the axis-ratio relation that splits the mean into Ex / Ey / Ez.
3. Compare against the literature fits as a sanity check (open-cell Voronoi 0.93·ρ^2.04, closed-cell 0.563·ρ^1.19, Roberts & Garboczi).
4. F13LD.foam estimator panel, laid out like F13LD.tpms, using the measured ρ the tool now shows live (v0.4.0, 48³ sample of the same field). Banner: "calibrated against F13LD.lab (n runs, date)". Results and the fit go in §6 here.

## 6. Results — first pass (Matt's GPU, 2026-10-03)

48 runs at Standard (1e-4), void 1e-6, 64 ↔ 128 pair, order 2, fast solver path (v0.15.0), 15–40 s per run. Results: [`foam-calibration/results_2026-10-03.csv`](foam-calibration/results_2026-10-03.csv). Analysis: open [`foam-calibration/foam-fit.html`](foam-calibration/foam-fit.html) and drop in the results CSV (charts, per-run table, fit JSON and Markdown export); `fit_foam.py` is the same fit in Python.

### 6.1 Laws (set A, Lloyd seeds, two realizations)

| | Law (E ÷ E_s, directional mean) | Range | Fit rms | ν |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Open** (and plateau, §6.3) | **0.724 · ρ^1.93** | 8–35 % | 3.3 % | 0.433 − 0.468 ρ |
| **Closed** | **0.304 · ρ + 0.456 · ρ²** | 12–35 % | 0.9 % | ≈ 0.29 |

- **Shear:** G = E / 2(1 + ν) to within 1–2 % on every run (median ratio 0.995), so only E and ν need laws.
- **Against the literature** (Roberts & Garboczi Voronoi fits): open within 3 % at 8–12 %, then progressively softer (−6 % at 18 %, −9 % at 25 %, −13 % at 35 %), consistent with this field putting extra mass at the nodes where it adds little bending stiffness; closed within 5 % across 12–35 %.
- **Scatter:** two seed realizations differ by ±3.2 % (open, 27 cells) and ±0.8 % (closed, 16 cells). Inside one tile the three axes spread ±7 % (open) and ±2 % (closed). Both shrink as the cell count rises; the tool's foams have 50–800 cells.
- **Seed mode:** Poisson-disk seeds are 0.956 × the Lloyd law (open) and 0.985 × (closed). Kelvin and Weaire–Phelan are **not calibrated**: their walls lie on voxel planes, so solid fraction and stiffness jump between 64³ and 128³ (Kelvin closed 12 % builds at 13.5 %; Kelvin open 12 % changes 1.8× between grids).
- **Stretch** (z, 18 %, against the same seeds unstretched): E_z / E_xy = s^2.49 (open), s^1.63 (closed); mean E stays within about 7 %. Shear splits as roughly (E_i E_j)^(r/2), r ≈ 0.57 open, ≈ 0.85 closed.

### 6.2 Resolution

Every run's 64³ → 128³ change was fitted to E_h = E∞(1 − a·w^−p), w = wall / strut width in voxels at 64³:

- **Open:** p ≈ 2.0–2.1 over all ten densities, so the lab's order-2 extrapolation matches the model within 1 % on every run and is used as is. The 64³ → 128³ change is 38 % at 8 % (2.9 voxels) and 3 % at 35 % (7 voxels).
- **Closed:** p ≈ 1.9–2.0 from the 25–35 % runs (walls converge faster than struts). At 12–18 % the 64³ walls are under 2 voxels and the 64³ solve is off that curve (one 12 % solve read E ≈ 0), so those points are the 128³ value ÷ (1 − a·w₁₂₈^−p): +13 % at 12 %, +5 % at 18 %. **Closed below 18 % is lower confidence.**
- Iterations: closed solves at 64³ hit the 300-iteration cap at 12–18 % (broken geometry); at 128³ they converge in 40–160 per load case. Open runs at 18 %+ stop at the cap with residuals 1.1–3 × 10⁻⁴; earlier Standard vs High comparisons agreed to 3 significant figures, so the stiffness is settled.

### 6.3 Plateau was not tested by set A

Set A's plateau rows are identical to the open rows from 12 % up (0 voxels differ at 18 %). The plateau swell `k·(1 − smoothstep(0, 0.35, d₃ − d₁))` only reaches 0.35 tile units in d₃ − d₁ from a strut's axis, and at 27 cells and 12 %+ the struts are thicker than that, so the swell sits inside solid. In the tool's typical foams (200 cells, thickness 0.08) the same k = 0.05 raises the solid from 4.0 % to 6.6 %. Plateau needs thin struts — §7.

## 7. Plateau pass (set P + N, 19 runs, Matt's GPU, 2026-10-03)

Run set [`foam-calibration/foam_plateau_runs.csv`](foam-calibration/foam_plateau_runs.csv) (`make_plateau_runs.js`); fit record [`fit_2026-10-03.json`](foam-calibration/fit_2026-10-03.json), [`fit_summary_2026-10-03.md`](foam-calibration/fit_summary_2026-10-03.md) (exported from the fit page, 67 runs).

**Plateau borders, read as each plateau foam ÷ its paired open foam (same cells, density, seeds) at 128³** — struts here are 1.2–2.4 voxels at 64³, so absolute values are loose but the resolution error cancels in the pair:

| k | 27 cells, 5 % | 50 cells, 5 % | 50 cells, 8 % |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.05 | — | 1.05 | 1.04 |
| 0.1 | 1.06 | 1.08 | 1.07 |
| 0.2 | 1.08 | 1.10 (repeat 1.12) | 1.10 (repeat 1.10) |
| 0.3 | 1.10 | — | 1.12 |

**Plateau factor at equal measured ρ: f = 1 + 0.118 (1 − e^(−k/0.111))**, rms 1.1 % over 12 pairs. It applies while the swell adds mass (set A, where it is buried inside thick struts, gives exactly 1); F13LD.foam scales it in over the first 5 % of added solid.

**Open law at 5 %:** the paired open foams sit 0.88–1.05 × the law — consistent within their (looser, thin-strut) uncertainty, so the open law is used from 5 %.

**Cell count (open, 12 %, one run each):** 0.96 × the law at 16 cells, 1.00 at 27 (two runs), 1.07 at 50. Not resolved by one run each, and the 50-cell open pairs at 5–8 % differ by 13–17 % between seeds (thin struts) — so §8.

## 9. F13LD.foam estimator (v0.5.0)

Uses §6–7: ρ measured by point-sampling the exact field at 64³ (worker threads, ~0.5–2 s); mean E from the open / closed law × Poisson-seed factor × plateau factor; ν(ρ); G = E/2(1+ν); stretch split s^m with the mean kept; shear split (E_i E_j)^(r/2). Likely range per axis combines the fit rms, seed-to-seed scatter scaled to the foam's cell count, the ±7 % cell-count band, and extra width for anything outside the calibration (density range, Kelvin / Weaire–Phelan, uniform-random seeds, organic > 0, normalize off, stretch beyond 2:1), each named in a "not calibrated" note. Exports a `homogenization` block with F13LD.tpms's field names (F13LD.mesh's foam summary shows it). Constants live in one `FOAM_CAL` block; refit = paste from the fit page's JSON.

## 10. Re-run at the 1000-iteration cap (v0.16.0) — ready to run

The first pass (§6) used the sweep's Standard preset, which then stopped each load case at 300 iterations. 27 of the 48 runs hit that cap: every open and plateau foam at 18–35 % on both grids, the 12–18 % closed foams at 64³ (their 128³ solves, which §6 uses, mostly converged), and the stretched (set D) foams. The solver starts from a uniform strain and works down, so a stopped solve reads **stiff**; the open law above 18 % may be high.

v0.16.0 raises Standard to 1000 iterations (tolerance unchanged, 1e-4) and the export records `cg_maxiter`. [`foam-calibration/foam_rerun_1000_runs.csv`](foam-calibration/foam_rerun_1000_runs.csv) holds the 27 rows, unchanged from `foam_calibration_runs.csv`; run them with the §1 settings (void 1e-6). In the fit page, drop the old results file **first** and the re-run results **after** — a run id loaded later replaces the earlier row. The plateau (§7) and cell-count (§8) files can be re-run as they are; they pick up the new cap automatically.
