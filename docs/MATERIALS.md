# F13LD.lab material library

45 entries across 9 families. Machine-readable version: `materials.json` (same order). Generated by `build_materials.py`, which holds the raw values, the source list and the Voce fit.

Values are **typical (mean) OEM or peer-reviewed values**, averaged over build orientations (XY/Z) unless the entry says otherwise. ASTM minimums are quoted separately in the notes. A dash means the value was not found; nothing was invented to fill it. Some ν, k, E and ρ values come from a wrought, bulk or family proxy because the AM source did not publish them. Each entry's 'Data flags' note says which.

## Voce calibration method

Hardening law used by the tool: σY(α) = σY0 + (σsat − σY0)(1 − e^(−δα)) + Hlin·α. The fit sets **Hlin = 0**.

1. **Uniform elongation.** No source in this set publishes uniform elongation (Ag), so every metal entry uses an estimate: e_u = min(0.8·e_break, e_break − 1 %). All of these are flagged *estimated*. For alloys with a low UTS/YS ratio (Ti-6Al-4V, maraging steel, AlSi10Mg), this rule likely overestimates e_u. A smaller real e_u would give a larger δ, meaning faster saturation.
2. **True UTS point.** σ_true,u = UTS·(1 + e_u); ε_p,u = ln(1 + e_u) − σ_true,u / E.
3. **Considère fit.** Solve σY(ε_p,u) = σ_true,u and dσY/dα(ε_p,u) = σ_true,u (necking onset). With A = σ_true,u − σY0 and S = σ_true,u, this reduces to e^(δ·ε_p,u) = 1 + (A/S)·δ, solved by bisection. Then σsat = S·(1 + 1/δ). A positive root exists only if A/S > ε_p,u.
4. **Fallback.** If A/S ≤ ε_p,u, δ = 3/ε_p,u, so 95 % of saturation is reached at the UTS strain, and σsat is set so the curve still passes through (ε_p,u, σ_true,u). This case is flagged `fallback95`. It is implemented, but no current entry triggers it: with the estimated e_u, every metal has a valid Considère root.
   Entries marked **`considere*`** are close to the limit (δ·ε_p,u < 1). For these, the fitted hardening is nearly linear up to the UTS, so σsat is an extrapolation well beyond the test data. This applies to high-ductility, high-hardening alloys such as 316L, IN625 and Hastelloy X. Keep crush strains moderate for them, or cap the flow stress.
5. **Degenerate.** If UTS ≤ σY0 or elongation is unknown, the material is treated as perfectly plastic (σsat = σY0). Only the PEEK FFF entry falls in this group.

## Caveats

- **Bulk coupons ≠ struts.** Every value here comes from machined or near-net tensile coupons at least a few mm across. Lattice struts (0.2–1 mm) usually show a lower effective modulus and strength. The reasons are surface roughness and partly fused powder that carry little load, a geometric deviation from CAD (thinner or thicker struts, waviness), notch-like surface defects, and near-surface porosity. Knockdowns of tens of percent relative to bulk are commonly reported for thin LPBF struts. No strut-level data were collected for this library. Calibrate with the as-built strut diameter (CT or measured), not the CAD value.
- **Orientation.** LPBF metals are typically stronger but less ductile in XY, and more ductile in Z. Ni alloys and 316L also show a lower Z modulus from <100> texture. Struts at 35–45° to the plate have the worst downskin roughness. The table averages XY and Z. Use the anisotropy notes for bounds.
- **As-built vs. HIP.** As-built Ti-6Al-4V is martensitic α′: about 1100 MPa yield and 8–10 % elongation, with high residual stress. Stress relief lowers strength slightly and raises ductility. HIP lowers yield by a further ~50–150 MPa relative to stress relief (see the Ti rows), closes internal porosity and generally improves fatigue, but does nothing for surface-connected roughness. CoCr and Ni alloys need solution treatment or HIP to become isotropic.
- **Porosity and chemistry.** OEM data assume at least 99.5 % density and virgin or controlled powder. Interstitials (O, N) strongly affect Ti, Ta and Nb strength. 17-4PH response depends on the atomization gas (retained austenite).
- **Proxies.** Poisson's ratio, thermal conductivity and sometimes E/ρ come from wrought or bulk data when the AM sheet omitted them. Thermal conductivity of AM AlSi10Mg depends strongly on heat treatment (120 → 170 W/m·K).
- **Voce is fitted to tension.** Lattice crush is compression- and bending-dominated. The fit captures hardening up to necking only. Post-necking damage and strut fracture are not modelled, which matters most for low-ductility entries (MS1, AlSi10Mg, PEEK/PEKK SLS).
